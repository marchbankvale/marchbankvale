01 — What we handle

Six things, done properly.

Most arrears cases are not lost at trial. They are lost months earlier, in a demand that was never validly served or a schedule that could not be reconciled.

01

Rent arrears recovery

From the first missed instalment to judgment. Reconciliation of the rent account, formal demand, a negotiated repayment plan where that is the commercial answer, and escalation where it is not. Assured shorthold · company let · licence

02

Ground rent & service charge arrears

Demands in the prescribed form under s.166 Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Act 2002, service charge demands within the s.20B time limit, and correspondence on breach of covenant. s.166 CLRA 2002 · s.20B LTA 1985 · breach of covenant

03

Statutory notices

Drafting and service of possession and breach notices under the current tenancy regime, with the evidence of service that makes them stand up when they are challenged. Housing Act 1988 as amended · Renters' Rights Act 2025

04

Pre-action protocol compliance

The Information Sheet, the Reply Form, the Financial Statement, the thirty-day clock, and the record that proves all four were provided. Non-compliance is the most common reason a claim is stayed or costs are refused. PAP for Debt Claims · CPR PD Pre-Action Conduct

05

Case preparation & bundles

Rent schedules, chronologies, witness statements, exhibit bundles and completed claim documentation, prepared for filing by you or by your solicitor. N1 · N119 · N325 · Money Claim Online

06

Instructing solicitors & counsel

Where a matter becomes contested or needs a regulated hand, we brief an external firm on your behalf and manage the file — so you deal with one point of contact, not three. Fixed-fee referral · file managed end to end

02 — The timeline

From instruction to a claim ready to issue in thirty-five days.

The protocol sets a thirty-day response window and we do not shorten it. What we compress is everything either side of it.

Day 0

Instruction

File opened, matter reference issued, and your authority to correspond recorded in writing.

Day 1–3

Audit

The tenancy, the lease and the rent account are reconciled. We find the defects now, not at the hearing.

Day 4

Letter before claim

Issued by post and email with the full prescribed enclosures and proof of posting retained.

Day 5–34

Response window

Thirty days. Where an offer arrives, we negotiate a documented repayment plan and monitor it.

Day 35+

Escalation

Bundle prepared and claim documentation completed for filing, or the file passed to instructed solicitors.

04 — Regulatory position

What we are, and what we are not.

We publish this on the website, on the letterhead and in the footer of every email we send. A recipient who checks will find exactly what we told them.

What we are

  • A specialist arrears recovery and litigation support practice, acting only on written instruction from the party entitled to the debt.
  • Experienced in the drafting, service and evidencing of demands and statutory notices in residential and leasehold property.
  • Able to prepare a file to the standard a county court expects, including schedules, statements and exhibit bundles.
  • Able to instruct and manage a regulated firm or counsel on your behalf where a matter requires it.

What we are not

  • Not a firm of solicitors. We are not authorised or regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority.
  • Not advisers on the law. We do not provide reserved legal services. Where advice is needed, we tell you so and arrange it.
  • Not litigators. We do not conduct litigation and we do not exercise rights of audience — both are reserved activities under the Legal Services Act 2007.
  • Not enforcement agents. We are not certificated bailiffs and we do not attend at properties to take control of goods.

Where arrears cases actually go wrong.

Written for landlords and freeholders, not for lawyers. Short, specific, and about the mistakes we see most often.

MV/N-021 · Possession

Ground 8 now needs three months, not two

The mandatory rent arrears ground has been reset. The threshold rose from two months to three, and the notice period doubled. Both change when a landlord can act.

8 September 2026 · 6 min MV/N-020 · Possession

Section 21 is gone. What actually changed on 1 May

Section 21 could last be served on 30 April 2026. On 1 May every assured shorthold tenancy converted to an assured periodic tenancy. The consequences run wider than evictions.

7 September 2026 · 5 min MV/N-018 · Litigation

Twenty-seven weeks, and what it should change about your approach

Ministry of Justice figures put the median time from possession claim to repossession at 27.1 weeks. That number should govern how arrears are handled long before a claim is issued.
